TraceFlow is a workspace for engineering teams building complex products. Requirements, tasks, tests, risks, documents and releases live in one linked graph, so you can follow any requirement to the work that built it, the tests that check it and the release that ships it. It runs in the browser and as an Android app.

## Who it is for

Teams that have to show their work: embedded and IoT products, automotive, medical devices, aerospace, robotics and industrial equipment. Anyone who keeps asking "is this requirement built, tested and shipped?" will recognise the problem.

## Modules

Each module works on its own and shares the same set of records, so nothing has to be synced by hand. Modules marked <em class="pro">Pro</em> are included from the Pro plan.

### Plan

<div class="module-list">

- **Requirements** Types, priority, status, ownership and parent and child hierarchies, organised by project and module.
- **Sprints** Plan sprints per module and follow progress from the tasks inside them.
- **Releases** <em class="pro">Pro</em> Plan and scope a release, with progress rolled up from every requirement inside it.

</div>

### Execute

<div class="module-list">

- **Tasks, bugs and to-dos** Epics, features, stories, bugs and quick items, each traceable back to a requirement.
- **Git linking** <em class="pro">Pro</em> Connect a GitHub or GitLab repository, and commits and pull requests that mention a task or requirement code link back on their own.
- **Time tracking** Log time against tasks and see it next to the estimate.
- **Comments and attachments** Discuss any record, and attach files to the discussion.
- **Notifications** Due-date reminders and updates on the work you follow.

</div>

### Verify

<div class="module-list">

- **Test management** <em class="pro">Pro</em> Test plans, cycles, cases, runs and results, linked to requirements so coverage is visible.
- **Risks** Severity and likelihood, with a mitigation note and an owner on every risk.
- **Traceability** <em class="pro">Pro</em> Links between requirements, tasks, tests and risks, with a coverage matrix, a mind map and export.

</div>

### Track

<div class="module-list">

- **Documents** Specs, designs and reports linked to the requirements they describe, as a link or as an uploaded file.
- **Activity and history** A readable, timestamped record of what changed on a project and who changed it.
- **Dashboard** Your tasks, sprint progress and what needs attention, the moment you sign in.
- **Reports** <em class="pro">Pro</em> Programme health, coverage and velocity, rolled up across a project.

</div>

## Roles and access

Work is organised as organisation, project, then module. People hold a role at the organisation and, separately, on each project.

| Level | Roles, highest first |
|---|---|
| Organisation | Admin, Manager, Developer, Viewer |
| Project | Project Owner, Maintainer, Contributor, Viewer |

An organisation Admin can manage every project in their organisation. People join by invitation and sign in with an email and password; signing up and resetting a password both use a six-digit code sent to the address. The apps hide what a role cannot do, and the data layer enforces it as well, so a hidden button is never the only safeguard.
